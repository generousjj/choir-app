  B-021 (p.7, 56-65, final barline). Every bar of the piece is visible. **No missing bars.**
- 80 measures written in performance order: 0a, 0b (intro bars "A","B"), 1-45, 5a-17a, then 44-65 (coda).
- Language: English.
- Tempo: no metronome mark, only "Brightly". I chose tempo 108 (quarter).
- Key/time: F major (-1). 18 is 2/4, 19 back to 4/4. D major (+2) from 24. Back to F at 5A (I used that through to the end).

## Structure / repeats
- Choir parts: segno at 5. After 43, D.S. al Coda. At 17 (the 2/4 bar 18) go to the Coda at 44.
- The piano-vocal score writes this out as 5A-17A (verse 3: "He rules the world"), then 44. I followed the
  written-out version, so: ... 39, 40-43, 5a-8a (rests), 9a-17a (verse 3), 44-65.
- Verse 2 (28-35) is "women in unison". The men are tacet and get rests. They come back on the last 8th of 35
  ("Re-peat the sounding joy"). The singer drew a pencil star there.
- 9A-17A look the same as 9-17 in the photos. I transcribed 9-17 in detail and copied them, checking 9A-11A,
  13A and 17A by eye.

## Which line
- Tenor and bass share one staff and are mostly printed as chords on a single stem (often 2-3 noteheads).
  I always took the **lowest** note. In 38-39 the parts split (tenor stems up, bass stems down) and I took the stems-down part.
- I wrote no divisi chords: no place showed the bass itself dividing.
- 54-55: one note per bar, so I assumed men in unison (C4, D4). 56-65: men in 2-3 notes (F4/D4, F4/C4, F4/C4/F3).
  I took the lowest. These are high bars for a bass (C4-D4 in 54-59).

## Doubts
1. m.38: the hardest bar. The stems-down notes are 8th F#3/D3, quarter D4/B3, 8th C#4/G3, then dotted 8th F#3,
   16th E3, 8ths D3 (with A3) and B2 (with G3). Taking the lowest note gives D3-B3-G3-F#3-E3-D3-B2, which has an odd
   leap D3 to B3. The upper chords may really be tenor, with no bass note printed. The lyric placement
   ("peat, re-peat__ the") is a best guess as well.
2. m.17 / 17a: I put "and"/"-ders" on the last 8th (F#3) and kept the G3 before it inside the "heav'n"/"won-" melisma,
   following where the soprano text sits.
3. m.9 / 9a, last 8th: Bb2 (it sits on the 4th line). It could be A2.
4. m.13-14 / 13a-14a: the bass sits high (A3-C4) under the tenor in tenths. Pitches look clear, but the melisma
   split (ev'=2 notes, ry=2, heart=3, pre=1) comes from the slurs and is a guess.
5. m.16 / 16a: I read "ture" as F3 then F#3 (one sharp on that note), and "And" as F-natural then E3 (16ths).
6. m.37: the second 16th of "sound-ing" is D3. Could be read as an upper voice.
7. m.57-59 and 61-64: the score prints a vowel "Ê" under the tied chords after "Joy". I treated these bars as tied
   continuations with no new syllable. Only the singer can say whether "Ê" is sung as a new syllable (re-attack)
   or is just a vowel instruction.
8. m.44: F3 half, then F3 and E3 quarters under "his". The bass may slur F3-E3 there. Tenor has C4-Bn3-Bb3.
9. Intro bars "A" and "B" are numbered 0a and 0b.
