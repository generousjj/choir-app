## Which line
The Vocal edition prints only one unison melody line (treble clef), with no separate men's part. I wrote that melody ONE OCTAVE LOWER
(the octave men sing). Range G2..E4 (lowest C3 in O Little Town m.206, highest E4/Eb4). If the singer was taught a
harmony part by ear, it is not on these pages.

## Doubts
1. Octave: I transposed the melody down an octave for the men. Only the singer can confirm that the men sing the tune and not their own harmony.
2. m.50 (Emmanuel): 6 quarter notes are printed with no time change ("Flowing, like a chant"), so I inserted 6/4 for that bar only.
3. m.110-121 (Here We Come refrain, cut time): no tempo is printed. I assumed half = 108 (quarter 216), keeping the 6/8 beat. It could be slower (for example half = 72).
4. m.109p (3/8) + m.110p (half-bar pickup): this is how the page prints it. These are not cut measures.
5. m.13: there is a slur over F#-E but two syllables "ye, O". I kept both syllables.
6. m.164: the printed natural E and F# (E-natural, F#) are kept.
7. m.195 (Deck, verse 4): "fa la la la la" is printed as C C C C Bb. instead of the earlier F G Ab F G. I followed the page.
8. O Little Town m.205/209/217: the low D (6th degree) on "-hem"/"sleep"/"years" and the low C on "thee" (m.206) were checked against the staff lines. They are clearly below the staff.
9. Here We Come m.105: Bb. tied to Bb on "green". I read the curve as a tie (same pitch).
